TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.BoundaryTemperature — Type
BoundaryTemperature(T_u)

User-defined boundary (wall) temperature from a function T_u(t, x, Ti) of the scaled time t, the scalar position x and the scaled mound material temperature Ti at x. It must return the scaled temperature (temperature in K divided by the reference temperature T_ref). Pass it as boundary_temperature to TermiteMoundEquations1D, e.g. a constant wall temperature of 300 K, with T_ref from termite_parameters:

boundary_temperature = BoundaryTemperature((t, x, Ti) -> 300.0 / T_ref)

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.BoundaryTemperatureTermiteMound — Type
BoundaryTemperatureTermiteMound(; xa, xb, xc, tᵣ, T_ref)

Scaled boundary (wall) temperature $T_u$ of the flow channel of a termite mound, defined piecewise:

\[T_u(t, x) = \begin{cases} T_{soil}(t) / T_{ref} & x \le x_a \text{ or } x > x_c \quad \text{(soil)}, \\ \frac{1}{2}\left(T_{air}(t) / T_{ref} + T_i(t, x)\right) & x_a < x \le x_b \quad \text{(flute)}, \\ T_i(t, x) & x_b < x \le x_c \quad \text{(chimney)}. \end{cases}\]

It depends on the scaled mound material temperature $T_i$, the soil temperature $T_{soil}$ (at 30 cm depth, T_soil) and the ambient air temperature $T_{air}$ (T_air). $T_{soil}$ and $T_{air}$ are daily cycles in K and are scaled by the reference temperature $T_{ref}$. The arguments are the scaled transitions xa, xb, xc, the reference time tᵣ (s) and the reference temperature T_ref (K), as returned by termite_parameters. Pass it as boundary_temperature to TermiteMoundEquations1D.

$T_{air}$ and $T_{soil}$ only depend on time. Therefore, the solvers compute them once per time point (T_air_soil) instead of once per node.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.CoupledSourceTerms — Type
CoupledSourceTerms(source_terms; ode_source_terms = nothing)

Select the "coupled" method, a coupled method of lines for the TME, when passed as source_terms to SemidiscretizationHyperbolic. The ODEs for $v$ and $p_0$ are part of the right-hand side Trixi.rhs! and are integrated together with the transport PDE for $\rho$ and the ODE for $T_i$ at every node by OrdinaryDiffEq (no operator splitting). The velocity $u$ is the algebraic variable of the system: it is computed from the velocity constraint in every right-hand side evaluation (every Runge-Kutta stage).

In every right-hand side evaluation:

  1. the state is copied into a buffer and the velocity $u = (v + I_0)/A$ is computed from it (compute_algebraic_velocity!),
  2. the Trixi right-hand side of $\rho$ and $T_i$ is evaluated on this state with the inner source_terms,
  3. the right-hand sides of $v$ and $p_0$ are evaluated (ode_rhs). Every node gets $\mathrm{d}p_0/\mathrm{d}t$ in the p field and $(\mathrm{d}v/\mathrm{d}t)/A$ in the v1 field ($v = A(x_{min}) \, v_1(x_{min})$ is stored at the first node).

Operators and work arrays are precomputed when the semidiscretization is created, so the right-hand side does not allocate.

Arguments

  • source_terms: source terms of the $\rho$ and $T_i$ equations (e.g. source_terms).
  • ode_source_terms: optional additional sources (x, t, equations) -> SVector(s_p, s_v) of the $p_0$ and $v$ equations (e.g. manufactured_source_terms_ode), or nothing (default) for no sources. These sources are only used for the manufactured solution.

Usage

Use it together with the AlgebraicVelocityCallback, placed before the StepsizeCallback:

semi = SemidiscretizationHyperbolic(mesh, equations, initial_condition, dg,
                                    source_terms = CoupledSourceTerms(source_terms),
                                    boundary_conditions = boundary_condition_periodic)
algebraic_velocity_callback = AlgebraicVelocityCallback()
callbacks = CallbackSet(summary_callback, algebraic_velocity_callback, stepsize_callback)
Note

For the "splitting" method, pass the plain source_terms instead and use the GodunovSplittingCallback.

  • $A > 0$ on $[0, 1]$,

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.CrossSectionSine — Type
CrossSectionSine(amplitude)

Cross section

\[A(x) = 1 + a \sin(2\pi x + \pi/2), \qquad A_x(x) = 2\pi a \cos(2\pi x + \pi/2),\]

with $a$ = amplitude. $A$ is $1$-periodic and largest at $x = 0$. The condition $|a| < 1$ is required for $A > 0$. For $a = 0$, it returns exactly $A = 1$ and $A_x = 0$. Pass it as cross_section to TermiteMoundEquations1D.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.GodunovSplittingCallback — Type
GodunovSplittingCallback(solver; source_terms = nothing)

Callback of the "splitting" method: a first-order Godunov (Lie-Trotter) operator splitting of the TME. Every time step $[t_{prev}, t_{now}]$ with $\Delta t = t_{now} - t_{prev}$ consists of two sub-steps:

  1. PDE step (Trixi): $\rho$ (transport PDE) and $T_i$ (ODE at every node, a source term in Trixi) are advanced from $t_{prev}$ to $t_{now}$ with $u$ and $p_0$ frozen (the v1 and p components of the Trixi right-hand side are zero).
  2. ODE step (this callback, called after every time step):
    • $v$ is advanced by a predictor-corrector (Heun) step: explicit Euler predictor $v^* = v_{prev} + \Delta t \, v'_{prev}$, then the trapezoidal rule

      \[v = v_{prev} + \frac{\Delta t}{2} \left( v'_{prev} + v'(\rho_{now}, u^*) \right),\]

      where $u^* = u_{prev} + (v^* - v_{prev})/A$ is the shifted velocity profile,
    • the velocity is computed from the velocity constraint,

      \[u = \frac{v + I_0}{A}, \qquad I_0(x) = \int_{x_{min}}^x A \, (Q - c) \, dy,\]

      with $Q = -I_w k_w (T - T_u) / (\sqrt{A} \gamma p_0)$ and $c = \int A Q \, dx / \int A \, dx$, evaluated at the state at $t_{prev}$. Only for the manufactured solution, the term $s_p / (\gamma p_0)$ with the source $s_p$ of the $p_0$ equation is added to $Q$,
    • $p_0$ is advanced by the trapezoidal rule with the rates p0_rhs at $t_{prev}$ and at $t_{now}$ (new $\rho$, $T_i$, $u$),
    • $u$ is written into the v1 field and $p_0$ into the p field at all nodes.

The auxiliary velocity $v = A(x_{min}) \, v_1(x_{min})$ is stored at the first node, where $I_0 = 0$. Integrals and derivatives use the DG operators of the solver (LGL quadrature, elementwise derivative and cumulative integral, see dg_operators). The splitting error is of first order in the time step.

Arguments

  • solver: ODE solver used for the integration of the (linearly interpolated) rates of $v$ and $p_0$. Since these rates are linear in time, any Runge-Kutta method gives the trapezoidal rule. We recommend CarpenterKennedy2N54 from OrdinaryDiffEqLowStorageRK.
  • source_terms: optional additional sources (x, t, equations) -> SVector(s_p, s_v) of the $p_0$ and $v$ equations (e.g. manufactured_source_terms_ode), or nothing (default) for no sources. These sources are only used for the manufactured solution.

Usage

Use it with the plain Trixi source_terms of $\rho$ and $T_i$ (e.g. source_terms):

godunov_splitting_callback =
    GodunovSplittingCallback(CarpenterKennedy2N54(williamson_condition = false))
callbacks = CallbackSet(summary_callback, stepsize_callback, godunov_splitting_callback)
Note

If the callback is not used, the quantities $u$, $v$ and $p_0$ remain constant in time. For the coupled method of lines without splitting error, see CoupledSourceTerms and AlgebraicVelocityCallback.

HeightProfileTermiteMound(; xa, xb, L, h)

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.TermiteMoundEquations1D — Type
TermiteMoundEquations1D(; γ, k_i, k_w, tᵣ, uᵣ, xa, xb, xc, r, h, L, β, η, Fr², ρₕ₀,
                        T_ref, t_ref, T0, v0, Ti_LI,
                        cross_section, height_profile, boundary_temperature)

Termite mound equations (TME) in one space dimension: a low Mach number model of the airflow through the closed flow channel of a termite mound (soil → flute → chimney → soil), parametrized by $x \in [0, 1]$ (periodic). All quantities are scaled.

Equations

\[\begin{aligned} \rho_t + (\rho u)_x &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u, \\ T_{i,t} &= k_i (T - T_i), \\ v'(t) &= \frac{1}{\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dx} \int_0^1 \left[ -\rho u u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \left(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\right) - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}) \right] dx, \\ p_0'(t) &= -\gamma p_0 \left(u_x + \frac{A_x}{A} u\right) - \frac{I_w k_w}{\sqrt{A}} \left(T - T_u(t, x, T_i)\right) \qquad \text{for all } x, \\ p_0 &= \rho T. \end{aligned}\]

Since $p_0$ does not depend on $x$, the $p_0$ equation determines the velocity. With $Q = -I_w k_w (T - T_u)/(\sqrt{A} \gamma p_0)$ and $c = \int_0^1 A Q \, dx / \int_0^1 A \, dx$:

\[p_0' = \gamma p_0 c, \qquad u(t, x) = \frac{v(t)}{A(x)} + \frac{1}{A(x)} \int_0^x A (Q - c) \, dy,\]

so that $v = A(0) u(t, 0)$ is the volume flux at $x = 0$.

Unknowns and state vector

The unknowns are the air density $\rho$, the air velocity $u$, the leading-order pressure $p_0$ (spatially constant), the mound material temperature $T_i$ and the auxiliary velocity $v$. The state vector at every node is (rho, v1, p, Ti) = $(\rho, u, p_0, T_i)$. The velocity $v$ is not stored separately (see GodunovSplittingCallback and CoupledSourceTerms).

Model data

The cross section, the height profile and the boundary temperature are passed as objects (required keyword arguments, see below), while the heat transfer factor is fixed:

  • cross section $A(x)$ and $A_x(x)$ (cross_section, see A),
  • slope of the height profile $h_x(x)$ (height_profile, see h_x),
  • boundary temperature $T_u(t, x, T_i)$ (boundary_temperature, see T_u),
  • spatially variable heat transfer coefficient $\alpha_w I_w(x)$ (I_w).

Usage

(γ, k_i, k_w, tᵣ, uᵣ, xa, xb, xc, r, h, L, β, η, Fr², ρₕ₀, T_ref, t_ref, T0, v0, Ti_LI) =
    termite_parameters(1.5, 2.5)
cross_section = CrossSectionConstant()
height_profile = HeightProfileTermiteMound(; xa, xb, L, h)
boundary_temperature = BoundaryTemperatureTermiteMound(; xa, xb, xc, tᵣ, T_ref)
equations = TermiteMoundEquations1D(; γ, k_i, k_w, tᵣ, uᵣ, xa, xb, xc, r, h, L, β, η, Fr²,
                                    ρₕ₀, T_ref, t_ref, T0, v0, Ti_LI,
                                    cross_section, height_profile, boundary_temperature)

Arguments

The scalar arguments are usually created by termite_parameters. Their defaults are those of termite_parameters(0.6, 2.0) (mound radius 0.6 m, height 2 m), except v0 = -2.219 and a simple three-point Ti_LI.

  • γ: adiabatic exponent.
  • k_i: heat exchange rate between air and mound material ($T_i$ equation).
  • k_w: scaled wall heat transfer coefficient ($p_0$ equation, contains $\alpha_w$).
  • tᵣ: reference time $L/u_r$ (s). uᵣ: reference velocity (m/s).
  • xa, xb, xc: scaled positions of the transitions soil → flute, flute → chimney (top of the mound) and chimney → soil (see mound_geometry).
  • r, h: radius and height of the mound (m). L: length of the flow channel (m).
  • β: friction coefficient. η: viscosity.
  • Fr²: squared Froude number $u_r^2/(gL)$. ρₕ₀: scaled reference density of the buoyancy term.
  • T_ref: reference temperature (K), used to scale temperatures. t_ref: its inverse $1/T_{ref}$ (1/K).
  • T0: initial air temperature (°C). v0: initial velocity (cm/s).
  • Ti_LI: initial mound material temperature (°C) as an interpolation object on $[0, 1]$.
  • cross_section (required): cross section of the flow channel, e.g. CrossSectionConstant ($A \equiv 1$), CrossSectionSine or a user-defined CrossSection.
  • height_profile (required): slope of the height profile, e.g. HeightProfileTermiteMound or a user-defined HeightProfile.
  • boundary_temperature (required): boundary (wall) temperature, e.g. BoundaryTemperatureTermiteMound or a user-defined BoundaryTemperature.
Note

The positions xa, xb, xc and the length L are used both by the equations ($I_w$, plotting of $T_i$) and by the termite mound height profile and boundary temperature. Create all of them from the same termite_parameters call.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.AlgebraicVelocityCallback — Method
AlgebraicVelocityCallback()

Callback of the "coupled" method (coupled method of lines, see CoupledSourceTerms). At initialization and after every time step, it writes the algebraic velocity

\[u = \frac{v + I_0}{A}, \qquad I_0(x) = \int_{x_{min}}^x A \, (Q - c) \, dy,\]

determined by the current state $(\rho, v, p_0, T_i)$, into the v1 field of the solution. Here $Q = -I_w k_w (T - T_u) / (\sqrt{A} \gamma p_0)$, $c = \int A Q \, dx / \int A \, dx$ and $T = p_0 / \rho$. Only for the manufactured solution, the term $s_p / (\gamma p_0)$ with the source $s_p$ of the $p_0$ equation is added to $Q$ (see CoupledSourceTerms). The auxiliary velocity $v = A(x_{min}) \, v_1(x_{min})$ is stored at the first node, where $I_0 = 0$.

The callback does not change the solution of the coupled system: the right-hand side reads only $v$ (first node), $\rho$, $p_0$ and $T_i$, and recomputes $u$ from the velocity constraint in every Runge-Kutta stage. The callback is needed because the StepsizeCallback (CFL condition), the AnalysisCallback and the output read the v1 field directly. Without it, they would see outdated velocities. At initialization it replaces the initial v1 (e.g. a constant $v_0$) by the velocity that satisfies the constraint.

Usage

Use it together with source_terms = CoupledSourceTerms(...) and place it before the StepsizeCallback and the AnalysisCallback:

algebraic_velocity_callback = AlgebraicVelocityCallback()
callbacks = CallbackSet(summary_callback, algebraic_velocity_callback, stepsize_callback,
                        analysis_callback)
Note

In contrast to the GodunovSplittingCallback of the "splitting" method, this callback does not advance $v$ and $p_0$ in time. This is done by the time integrator. An error is thrown if the semidiscretization was not created with CoupledSourceTerms.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.Get_initial_Ti — Method
Get_initial_Ti(r, h; n_samples = 513)

Initial profile of the mound material temperature (°C) of a mound with radius r and height h (both in m). The steps are:

  1. The four profile parameters (Ta, Tb, Tc, k) of Ti_profile are bilinearly interpolated in (r, h) from the table TI_PARAMS on the grid $r \in \{0.3, 0.6, \dots, 1.5\}$, $h \in \{0.5, 1.0, \dots, 2.5\}$. Outside of the grid, r and h are clamped to it.
  2. The positions xa, xb, xc follow from mound_geometry.
  3. The profile is sampled at n_samples equidistant points in $[0, 1]$ plus xa, xb, xc (so that the kinks are represented exactly).

The table contains least-squares fits to quasi-periodic states of multi-day simulations.

Arguments

  • r: radius of the mound (m).
  • h: height of the mound (m).
  • n_samples: number of equidistant sample points. Default: 513.

Returns

A linear interpolation object on $[0, 1]$ (constant extrapolation), stored as Ti_LI in TermiteMoundEquations1D.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.I_w — Method
I_w(x, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Spatial factor $I_w(x)$ of the wall heat transfer coefficient at the scalar position x. It makes the heat transfer coefficient $\alpha_w$ variable in space: the effective coefficient at $x$ is $\alpha_w I_w(x)$. Since $k_w$ is proportional to $\alpha_w$ (see termite_parameters), the heat transfer term of the $p_0$ equation reads $-I_w(x) \, k_w (T - T_u)/\sqrt{A}$.

\[I_w(x) = \begin{cases} 0.5 & x \le x_a \text{ or } x > x_c \quad \text{(soil)}, \\ 1 & x_a < x \le x_c \quad \text{(mound: flute and chimney)}. \end{cases}\]

So the heat transfer in the soil sections is half of that inside the mound.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.ManufacturedInitialCondition — Method
ManufacturedInitialCondition(x, t, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Exact (manufactured) solution of the forced TME (see manufactured_fields) as the state vector (rho, v1, p, Ti) = $(\rho, u, p_0, T_i)$ at the position x (vector with one entry) and time t. Used as the initial condition ($t = 0$) and as the reference solution for the errors.

At $t = 0$ the initial condition is

\[\begin{aligned} \rho(0, x) &= \rho_0 \left(1 + \varepsilon_\rho \sin(2\pi x)\right), & u(0, x) &= \frac{v_0 + \varepsilon_u \sin(2\pi x)}{A(x)}, \\ p_0(0) &= 1, & T_i(0, x) &= T_{i,0} \left(1 + \varepsilon_T \cos(2\pi x)\right), \end{aligned}\]

so that $v(0) = A(0) \, u(0, 0) = v_0$. The constants are defined in manufactured_fields.

Use it together with manufactured_source_terms (sources of $\rho$ and $T_i$) and manufactured_source_terms_ode (sources of $p_0$ and $v$), for the "splitting" (GodunovSplittingCallback) and the "coupled" (CoupledSourceTerms) methods.

Returns

SVector(rho, v1, p, Ti).

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.TermiteMoundInitialCondition — Method
TermiteMoundInitialCondition(x, t, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Initial state of the termite mound simulations at the position x (vector with one entry), independent of the time t:

\[\rho = \frac{1}{T_0}, \qquad u = v_0, \qquad p_0 = 1, \qquad T_i = T_{i,LI}(x),\]

with the scaled initial air temperature $T_0$ (from T0 in °C), the scaled initial velocity $v_0$ (from v0 in cm/s) and the scaled initial mound material temperature profile Ti_LI (in °C). Hence $T = p_0/\rho = T_0$ everywhere.

The constant velocity $u = v_0$ does not satisfy the velocity constraint in general. The "splitting" method replaces it after the first time step, the "coupled" method at initialization (AlgebraicVelocityCallback).

Returns

SVector(rho, v1, p, Ti).

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.Ti_profile — Method
Ti_profile(x, Ta, Tb, Tc, k, xa, xb, xc)

Initial mound material temperature (°C) at position x:

\[T_i(x) = \begin{cases} T_a & x \le x_a \quad \text{(soil)}, \\ T_a + (T_b - T_a) \dfrac{x - x_a}{x_b - x_a} & x_a < x \le x_b \quad \text{(flute)}, \\[1ex] T_c + (T_b - T_c) \dfrac{e^{-k\eta} - e^{-k}}{1 - e^{-k}}, \quad \eta = \dfrac{x - x_b}{x_c - x_b} & x_b < x \le x_c \quad \text{(chimney)}, \\[1ex] T_c & x > x_c \quad \text{(soil)}. \end{cases}\]

The profile rises linearly from $T_a$ at $x_a$ to the maximum $T_b$ at the top of the mound $x_b$, then decays to $T_c$ at $x_c$ with the decay rate $k$. The decay is linear for k < 1e-8. Outside of $(x_a, x_c]$, the profile is constant, since $T_i$ does not enter the model there.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.compute_algebraic_velocity! — Method
compute_algebraic_velocity!(u, t, equations, ode_source_terms, tc)

Overwrite the v1 field of the state vector u (the node values of the velocity $u$, except at the first node) with the algebraic velocity $u = (v + I_0(x)) / A$ determined by the current state, where

\[v = A(x_{min}) v_1(x_{min}), \quad I_0(x) = \int_{x_{min}}^x A (Q - c) \, dy, \quad Q = \frac{-I_w k_w (T - T_u)/\sqrt{A} + s_p}{\gamma p_0}, \quad c = \frac{\int A Q \, dx}{\int A \, dx}.\]

  • The source $s_p$ of the $p_0$ equation is only used for the manufactured solution ($s_p = 0$ otherwise).
  • $T_u$ is evaluated with the time-only part computed once (T_u_time, T_u_node, see T_u).
  • $I_0$ is the exact integral of the elementwise interpolation polynomial of $A (Q - c)$ (elements from left to right), so $I_0 = 0$ at the first node, and v1 there stays exactly the stored value.
  • The work arrays Q, T, Tu, s_p, s_v, v1 and v1_dx (DG derivative of the velocity) of the cache tc are filled for ode_rhs.

Used by the right-hand side of CoupledSourceTerms and by the AlgebraicVelocityCallback.

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.manufactured_fields — Method
manufactured_fields(x, t, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Exact fields and derivatives of the manufactured solution of the TME at the point x (a scalar) and time t, in scaled variables.

Forced problem

On the periodic domain $x \in [0, 1]$, the manufactured solution solves the TME with additional forcing terms $s_\rho, s_T, s_p, s_v$:

\[\begin{aligned} \rho_t + (\rho u)_x &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u + s_\rho, \\ T_{i,t} &= k_i (T - T_i) + s_T, \\ p_0'(t) &= -\gamma p_0 \left(u_x + \frac{A_x}{A} u\right) - \frac{I_w k_w}{\sqrt{A}} \left(T - T_u(t, x, T_i)\right) + s_p \qquad \text{for all } x, \\ \left(\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dx\right) v'(t) &= \int_0^1 \left[ -\rho u u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \left(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\right) - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}) + s_v \right] dx, \\ T &= \frac{p_0}{\rho}, \qquad A(0) \, u(t, 0) = v(t). \end{aligned}\]

The $p_0$ equation holds pointwise. Since $p_0$ does not depend on $x$, it is equivalent to the velocity constraint $A u = v + \int_0^x A (Q - c) \, dy$ (with the additional term $s_p / (\gamma p_0)$ in $Q$) together with the ODE $p_0' = \gamma p_0 c$. The sources are given in manufactured_source_terms ($s_\rho, s_T$) and manufactured_source_terms_ode ($s_p, s_v$).

Data

The model data of equations are used unchanged, so the manufactured solution works with any cross section, height profile and boundary temperature:

  • cross section $A(x)$, $A_x(x)$ (equations.cross_section, see A), where the examples use CrossSectionSine(0.3), i.e. $A(x) = 1 + 0.3 \sin(2\pi x + \pi/2)$, so that all $A$ and $A_x$ terms are active,
  • the slope $h_x$ (equations.height_profile, see h_x) and the heat transfer factor $I_w$ (I_w),
  • the boundary temperature $T_u(t, x, T_i)$ (equations.boundary_temperature, see T_u).

For the manufactured solution, $A$ must be $1$-periodic and positive.

Manufactured fields

With $\omega = 2\pi/10$ (one period on $t \in [0, 10]$) and $k = 2\pi$:

\[\begin{aligned} \rho &= \rho_0 \left(1 + \varepsilon_\rho \sin(kx + \omega t)\right), & p_0 &= 1 + \varepsilon_p \sin(\omega t), \\ v &= v_0 \left(1 + \varepsilon_v \sin(\omega t)\right), & T_i &= T_{i,0} \left(1 + \varepsilon_T \cos(kx - \omega t)\right), \\ u &= \frac{v + w}{A}, & w &= \varepsilon_u \left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2} \sin(\omega t)\right) \sin(kx), \end{aligned}\]

with $\varepsilon_\rho = 10^{-3}$, $\varepsilon_p = 10^{-3}$, $\varepsilon_v = 0.2$, $\varepsilon_u = 0.1$, $\varepsilon_T = 2 \cdot 10^{-3}$. The base values are those of the physical initial state: $\rho_0 = 1/T_0$ and $T_{i,0} = T_0$ with the scaled T0, and $v_0$ the scaled v0.

These choices have the following properties:

  • all fields are smooth and $1$-periodic in $x$, and $p_0$ only depends on $t$,
  • $A u = v + w$ is periodic and $w(t, 0) = 0$, so $A(0) u(t, 0) = v(t)$ holds exactly,
  • $u < 0$ everywhere (no sign change in $|u|$ or in flux_ranocha),
  • $u_x = (w_x - A_x u)/A$ follows from $(A u)_x = w_x$.
Note

$\varepsilon_\rho$ must stay small. A velocity perturbation $\delta v$ advects the density gradient ($\delta\rho_t \approx -\delta v \, \rho_x / A$), which feeds back into $v$ through the buoyancy term $h_x \delta\rho / Fr^2$ ($Fr^2 \sim 5 \cdot 10^{-5}$). For $\varepsilon_\rho = 10^{-2}$, this makes the manufactured trajectory unstable during part of each period (top-heavy density stratification), and the error grows to $O(1)$ independently of the mesh size and the time step.

Returns

A NamedTuple with the fields and the derivatives needed for the source terms: rho, rho_t, rho_x, p0, p0_t, v, v_t, u, u_x, Ti, Ti_t, and A_, A_x_ ($A$ and $A_x$ at x).

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.manufactured_source_terms — Method
manufactured_source_terms(u, x, t, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Source terms of the $\rho$ and $T_i$ equations of the forced TME (handled by Trixi, see manufactured_fields): the model source terms source_terms, evaluated on the numerical state u at the position x (vector with one entry), plus the manufactured sources $s_\rho$ and $s_T$, evaluated on the exact fields.

The forced problem reads as follows (the $p_0$ and $v$ sources are given by manufactured_source_terms_ode):

\[\begin{aligned} \rho_t + (\rho u)_x &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u + s_\rho, \\ T_{i,t} &= k_i (T - T_i) + s_T, \\ p_0' &= -\gamma p_0 \left(u_x + \frac{A_x}{A} u\right) - \frac{I_w k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) + s_p \qquad \text{for all } x, \\ \left(\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dx\right) v' &= \int_0^1 \left[ F_v + s_v \right] dx, \end{aligned}\]

with $T = p_0/\rho$ and the momentum integrand $F_v = -\rho u u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u (\eta + (1-\eta)|u|) - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2}(\rho - \rho_{h_0})$. Inserting the exact fields $\hat\rho, \hat u, \hat p_0, \hat T_i$ (with $\hat T = \hat p_0 / \hat\rho$) gives the sources

\[\begin{aligned} s_\rho &= \hat\rho_t + \hat\rho_x \hat u + \hat\rho \hat u_x + \frac{A_x}{A} \hat\rho \hat u, \\ s_T &= \hat T_{i,t} - k_i (\hat T - \hat T_i). \end{aligned}\]

Returns

source_terms(u, x, t, equations) + SVector(s_rho, 0, 0, s_Ti). The v1 and p components stay zero: they are updated by the ODE part of the system (GodunovSplittingCallback or CoupledSourceTerms).

TermiteMoundInducedAirflowTrixi.manufactured_source_terms_ode — Method
manufactured_source_terms_ode(x, t, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Manufactured sources $(s_p, s_v)$ of the $p_0$ and $v$ equations of the forced TME (see manufactured_fields) at the position x (a scalar) and time t. With the exact fields $\hat\rho, \hat u, \hat p_0, \hat v, \hat T_i$, $\hat T = \hat p_0 / \hat\rho$ and $\hat T_u = T_u(t, x, \hat T_i)$:

\[\begin{aligned} s_p &= \hat p_0' - \left[ -\gamma \hat p_0 \hat u_x - \gamma \frac{A_x}{A} \hat u \hat p_0 - \frac{I_w k_w}{\sqrt{A}} \left(\hat T - \hat T_u\right) \right], \\ s_v &= \frac{\hat\rho}{A} \hat v' - \hat F_v, \qquad \hat F_v = -\hat\rho \hat u \hat u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \hat\rho \hat u \left(\eta + (1-\eta)|\hat u|\right) - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\hat\rho - \rho_{h_0}). \end{aligned}\]

  • $s_p$ is added pointwise to the right-hand side of the $p_0$ equation, and hence, as $s_p / (\gamma p_0)$, also to $Q$ in the velocity constraint. It must depend on $x$: only then can the manufactured velocity $\hat u$ be chosen freely and smoothly. $s_p$ is uniquely determined by the fields.
  • $s_v$ is added to the integrand of the $v$ equation. Only its integral matters. The pointwise choice above makes the discrete integrand equal to $\hat\rho \hat v' / A$ at every node, so the $v$ equation is reproduced without quadrature error.

Usage

Pass it as source_terms to GodunovSplittingCallback ("splitting") or as ode_source_terms to CoupledSourceTerms ("coupled").

Returns

SVector(s_p, s_v).

Trixi.flux_hllc — Method
flux_hllc(u_ll, u_rr, orientation, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Surface flux: the mass component of the HLLC flux (HLL with contact) of the compressible Euler equations by E. F. Toro, evaluated with $(\rho, u, p_0)$ ($p_0$ as pressure, total energy $p_0/(\gamma - 1) + \rho u^2/2$, sound speed $\sqrt{\gamma p_0/\rho}$). The signal speeds are the Einfeldt estimates with Roe averages, DOI: 10.1137/S1064827593260140.

Only the mass component is computed, since only the $\rho$ equation has a flux. It needs:

  • the signal speeds $S_L = \min(u_{ll} - c_{ll}, \tilde u - \tilde c)$ and $S_R = \max(u_{rr} + c_{rr}, \tilde u + \tilde c)$ with the Roe averages $\tilde u$ and $\tilde c$ (the latter from the Roe-averaged enthalpy, hence the total energy),
  • the contact speed $S^*$ and the density $\rho^*$ of the star region on each side of the interface.

The flux is then

\[f_1 = \begin{cases} \rho_{ll} u_{ll} & S_L \ge 0, \\ \rho_{rr} u_{rr} & S_R \le 0, \\ \rho_{ll} u_{ll} + S_L (\rho^*_{ll} - \rho_{ll}) & S_L \le 0 \le S^*, \\ \rho_{rr} u_{rr} + S_R (\rho^*_{rr} - \rho_{rr}) & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad \rho^*_K = \rho_K \frac{S_K - u_K}{S_K - S^*}.\]

The momentum and energy components of the Euler HLLC flux are not computed. The upwinding through the acoustic signal speeds provides the numerical dissipation at element interfaces.

A second method with a normal_direction::AbstractVector rotates the velocity to the normal direction, calls this method and scales the result by the length of the normal vector.

Returns

SVector(f1, 0, 0, 0).

flux_ranocha(u_ll, u_rr, orientation, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Two-point volume flux for the flux differencing volume integral (VolumeIntegralFluxDifferencing), built from logarithmic means as in Ranocha's flux for the Euler equations. Only the mass flux is nonzero:

\[f_1 = \rho^{\ln} \, \bar u, \qquad \bar u = \begin{cases} u^{\ln} & u_{ll}, u_{rr} > 0, \\ -(-u)^{\ln} & u_{ll}, u_{rr} < 0, \\ \frac{1}{2}(u_{ll} + u_{rr}) & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}\]

where $a^{\ln}$ is the logarithmic mean of the left and right values (Trixi's ln_mean). The logarithmic mean of the velocity is only defined for values of the same sign, so the arithmetic mean is used if the sign changes. The flux is consistent: $f_1 = \rho u$ for $u_{ll} = u_{rr}$.

Convert primitive variables (rho, v1, p, Ti) to the variables of the state vector. Both are identical, so this is the identity and returns u unchanged.

source
Trixi.varnames — Method
Trixi.varnames(conversion_function, equations::TermiteMoundEquations1D)

Names of the four variables of the state vector, used for output and plotting. The names are the same for every conversion_function, since the state is stored in primitive variables.

Returns

("rho", "v1", "p", "Ti"): density $\rho$, velocity $u$, leading-order pressure $p_0$ and mound material temperature $T_i$.
