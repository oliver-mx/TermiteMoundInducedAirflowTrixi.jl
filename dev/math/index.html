Mathematics

The 1D termite mound model originates from the Euler equations of gas dynamics in a low Mach number regime. This page summarizes the derivation. More details can be found in the corresponding publication.

Scaled Euler equations

A scaled version of the Euler equations in one spatial dimension with the ideal gas law reads

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u, \\ \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ \rho u^2 + \frac{1}{\epsilon} p \right] &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u^2 - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \big(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\big) - \frac{1}{Fr^2} \rho h_x, \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left[ \rho T + \epsilon (\gamma - 1) \left( \frac{\rho u^2}{2} + \frac{\rho h}{Fr^2} \right) \right] \\ + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ \rho u T + (\gamma - 1) \left( \epsilon \left( \frac{\rho u^2}{2} + \frac{\rho u h}{Fr^2} \right) + u p \right) \right] &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u T - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) \\ &\hphantom{=} - (\gamma - 1) \frac{A_x}{A} \left( \epsilon \left( \frac{\rho u^2}{2} + \frac{\rho u h}{Fr^2} \right) + u p \right), \\ p &= \rho T. \end{aligned}\]

Unknowns:

  • density $\rho$,
  • velocity $u$,
  • pressure $p$,
  • temperature $T$.

Variables:

  • space $x$,
  • time $t$.

Functions (model data):

  • cross section of the flow channel $A$,
  • height profile $h$,
  • boundary temperature $T_u$.

Parameters:

  • adiabatic exponent $\gamma = \frac{c_v + R}{c_v}$,
  • Mach number $M = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_r u_r^2}{\gamma p_r}}$,
  • Froude number $Fr = \frac{u_r}{\sqrt{g L}}$,
  • $\epsilon = \gamma M^2 = \frac{\rho_r u_r^2}{p_r}$,
  • pipe friction coefficient $\lambda_w = \frac{64}{Rey}$,
  • Reynolds number $Rey = \frac{u D_0 \rho}{\eta}$,
  • viscosity $\eta$,
  • friction coefficient $\beta = \frac{\lambda_w x_r \sqrt{\pi}}{4 \sqrt{A_r}}$,
  • wall heat transfer coefficient $k_w = (\gamma - 1) \frac{x_r \alpha_w T_r \sqrt{\pi}}{u_r p_r \sqrt{A_r}}$.

The boundary temperature is defined piecewise by

\[T_u(t, x) = \begin{cases} T_{soil}(t) & x \in [0, x_a] \cup (x_c, 1] \quad \text{(soil)}, \\ \frac{1}{2} \left( T_{air}(t) + T_i(t, x) \right) & x \in (x_a, x_b] \quad \text{(flute)}, \\ T_i(t, x) & x \in (x_b, x_c] \quad \text{(chimney)}. \end{cases}\]

It depends on the mound material temperature $T_i$, the soil temperature $T_{soil}$ (at 30 cm depth) and the ambient air temperature $T_{air}$. The functions $T_{air}$ and $T_{soil}$ (daily cycles) and the positions $x_a$, $x_b$ and $x_c$ are known. The mound material temperature $T_i$ is an additional unknown. Its evolution is described by

\[\frac{\partial T_i}{\partial t} = k_i (T - T_i).\]

New unknown:

  • mound material temperature $T_i$.

New parameter:

  • heat exchange rate $k_i = \frac{t_r \kappa L_i}{c_i m}$ with the thermal conductivity $\kappa$, the specific heat capacity $c_i$ and the mass $m$ of the mound material, and the length $L_i$ of the channel inside the mound.

Asymptotic model

In the scaled equations, the parameter $\epsilon$ is problematic. For a termite mound, $\epsilon$ is small and the equations become stiff and expensive to solve. Therefore, we consider the asymptotic expansion with leading order $O(\epsilon^0)$

\[\begin{aligned} \rho &= \rho_0 + O(\epsilon), \\ u &= u_0 + O(\epsilon), \\ T &= T_0 + O(\epsilon), \\ p &= p_0 + p_h + \epsilon p_1 + O(\epsilon^2). \end{aligned}\]

Inserting this expansion gives

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u, \\ \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ \rho u^2 + p_1 \right] &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u^2 - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \big(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\big) \\ &\hphantom{=} - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}), \\ \frac{\partial p_0}{\partial t} + \gamma p_0 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} &= -\gamma \frac{A_x}{A} u p_0 - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u), \\ \frac{\partial p_0}{\partial x} &= 0, \\ p_0 &= \rho T, \\ \frac{\partial T_i}{\partial t} &= k_i (T - T_i). \end{aligned}\]

Unknowns:

  • density $\rho$,
  • velocity $u$,
  • leading-order pressure $p_0$,
  • first-order pressure $p_1$,
  • air temperature $T$,
  • mound material temperature $T_i$.

Due to the closed flow channel, we impose periodic boundary conditions. As a result, $p_0$ is constant in space. We assume appropriate initial states $\rho^0(x)$, $u^0(x)$, $p_0^0$ and $T_i^0(x)$. The boundary conditions are $p_1(t, 0) = 0$, $p_1(t, 1) = 0$ and $A(0) \rho(t, 0) u(t, 0) = A(1) \rho(t, 1) u(t, 1)$.

Stationary case

In the stationary case, we make two assumptions for the mound material temperature. First, $T_i$ is constant in $x$. Second, the ordinary differential equation (ODE) for $T_i$ is replaced by an algebraic equation, assuming that the heat flux of the stationary system is in equilibrium. The stationary equations are

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u, \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ \rho u^2 + p_1 \right] &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u^2 - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \big(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\big) \\ &\hphantom{=} - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}), \\ \gamma p_0 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} &= -\gamma \frac{A_x}{A} u p_0 - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u), \\ \frac{\partial p_0}{\partial x} &= 0, \\ p_0 &= \rho T, \\ 0 &= \int_0^1 \frac{k_w}{\gamma \sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) \, dx. \end{aligned}\]

With appropriate initial and boundary conditions, $p_0 = 1$.

Unknowns:

  • density $\rho$,
  • velocity $u$,
  • first-order pressure $p_1$,
  • air temperature $T$,
  • mound material temperature $T_i$ (a scalar).

The stationary system can be solved as a differential-algebraic equation (DAE).

Reformulation for the numerical solution

The $p_0$ equation of the asymptotic model can be written as an equation for the spatial variation of the velocity,

\[\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = -\frac{A_x}{A} u + Q, \qquad Q = \frac{1}{\gamma p_0} \left[ -\frac{\mathrm{d} p_0}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) \right],\]

i.e. $(A u)_x = A Q$. Integrating from $0$ to $x$ gives the velocity constraint

\[u(t, x) = \frac{v(t)}{A(x)} + \frac{1}{A(x)} \int_0^x \frac{A(y)}{\gamma p_0} \left[ -\frac{\mathrm{d} p_0}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) \right] dy,\]

with the new variable $v(t) = A(0) u(t, 0)$, the volume flux at $x = 0$. Since $A u$ is periodic, the integral over the whole domain vanishes. This determines the time derivative of the pressure:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d} p_0}{\mathrm{d} t} = -\gamma p_0 \, \frac{\int_0^1 \frac{k_w \sqrt{A}}{\gamma p_0} (T - T_u) \, dx}{\int_0^1 A \, dx}.\]

The variable $v$ satisfies the ODE

\[\frac{\mathrm{d} v}{\mathrm{d} t} = \frac{1}{\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dy} \int_0^1 \left[ -\rho u u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \big(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\big) - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}) \right] dy,\]

which is obtained by integrating the momentum balance of the asymptotic model over the domain. The periodic boundary conditions of $p_1$ eliminate the variable $p_1$.

Finally, the reformulated asymptotic model, i.e. the 1D termite mound model, reads

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} &= -\frac{A_x}{A} \rho u, \\ \frac{\partial T_i}{\partial t} &= k_i (T - T_i), \\ \frac{\partial p_0}{\partial t} + \gamma p_0 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} &= -\gamma \frac{A_x}{A} u p_0 - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u), \\ \frac{\mathrm{d} v}{\mathrm{d} t} &= \frac{1}{\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dy} \int_0^1 \Big[ -\rho u u_x - \frac{\beta}{A} \rho u \big(\eta + (1-\eta)|u|\big) \\ &\hphantom{= \frac{1}{\int_0^1 \frac{\rho}{A} \, dy} \int_0^1 \Big[} - \frac{h_x}{Fr^2} (\rho - \rho_{h_0}) \Big] \, dy, \\ p_0 &= \rho T, \\ u(t, x) &= \frac{v(t)}{A(x)} + \frac{1}{A(x)} \int_0^x \frac{A(y)}{\gamma p_0} \left[ -\frac{\mathrm{d} p_0}{\mathrm{d} t} - \frac{k_w}{\sqrt{A}} (T - T_u) \right] dy. \end{aligned}\]

Unknowns:

  • density $\rho$,
  • velocity $u$,
  • auxiliary velocity $v$,
  • leading-order pressure $p_0$,
  • air temperature $T$,
  • mound material temperature $T_i$.
